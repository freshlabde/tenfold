tenfold · the mark

A ten, drawn
as an instrument.

The 0 is closed and whole: the ten goals, the shape of a life, one thing you can hold. The 1 is the same stroke creased twice - not a bar but a rod folded into three levels, the one goal you are inside, unfolded until a step small enough to do falls out of the bottom.

The whole idea is in two moves and no illustration. Nothing here is a target, a tick or an arrow; nothing needs colour to be understood; and at sixteen pixels the creases close, the mark says 10, and no meaning is lost.

Construction

Two weights, one optical.

The ring is drawn at 60 against the stem's 56. That is not a mistake and not arithmetic: a curve of equal width reads lighter than a straight, and the four units are what it takes for the two to look the same weight. Everything else is symmetrical about the centre.

The size ladder

Every mark below is the shipping geometry, re-drawn at that size - not one image scaled. The creases are a reward for the size that can hold them, never a condition for reading the mark.

Reproduction

Round one — five directions

Drawn, then judged at 16 and 32 pixels rather than at 512, which is where four of the five died. The note under each is the reason, not a rationalisation after the fact.

Round two — what a ten can be

The numeral survived, so the question became which parts of it carry the folding. Eight were drawn; these are the ones worth keeping on the page.

Round three — the crease

One number left to settle. Too fine and the fold is invisible even at 512; too open and the stem stops being a stroke and becomes three dots - the mark reads :O. Ten units is the width at which the crease is unmistakable on a home screen and closes to a whisper in a tab.
